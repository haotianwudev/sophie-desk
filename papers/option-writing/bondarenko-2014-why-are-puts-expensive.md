# Why Are Put Options So Expensive?

- **Authors:** Oleg Bondarenko
- **Year:** 2014 (Quarterly Journal of Finance 4(3), 1450015; working paper 2003)
- **Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1142/S2010139214500153](https://doi.org/10.1142/S2010139214500153)
- **PDF:** `bondarenko-2014-why-are-puts-expensive.pdf` — the open 2003 working-paper draft

## Testable Hypothesis

The high abnormal returns generated by selling out-of-the-money S&P 500 put options represent a statistical arbitrage that cannot be rationalized by standard equilibrium asset-pricing models, even after accounting for peso problems, unobserved jump regimes, or generalized state-dependent utility functions.

## Summary

Applies a non-parametric, model-free statistical arbitrage testing methodology to historical S&P 500 index option prices from 1987 to 2000. Shows that simple mechanical strategies of selling monthly at-the-money and out-of-the-money put options generate average annualized excess returns exceeding 40% with high Sharpe ratios. Proves that this "put anomaly" violates no-arbitrage bounds for any path-independent pricing kernel and cannot be attributed to sample selection bias (the Peso problem) or rational risk premia within standard expected utility or jump-diffusion frameworks.

## Relevance to Personal Trading & Research

- **Rating:** High
- **Rationale:** Foundational empirical validation for short put strategies, demonstrating that selling OTM SPX puts produces annualized excess returns >40% that violate no-arbitrage bounds for any path-independent pricing kernel. Confirms that put overpricing is not merely a statistical artifact of unobserved disaster risks (Peso problem) but an exploitable structural premium.
